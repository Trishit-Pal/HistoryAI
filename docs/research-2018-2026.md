# Research proposal: exhibits for 2018 to 1 Nov 2026 (proposal, nothing changed yet)

Scope: new exhibits for the Transformer era, Generative boom and Frontier rooms, covering 2018 to 2026-11-01. They do not duplicate the existing exhibits: AlexNet, GANs, Tay, AlphaGo, `evt_2017_transformer`, `evt_2020_alphafold`, `evt_2022_chatgpt`, CAIS, the EU AI Act and the 2024 Nobels.

The bar is the existing confirmed rule (`src/domain/confidence.ts`). It needs `officialPrimary === true`, meaning one citation is the paper, the model card or system card, or the lab's own announcement. It also needs citations from at least 2 distinct hostnames, with `www.` stripped. Every URL below was opened on 2026-11-04. Where a page could not be opened, the notes say so. Every citation's `retrieved` is `2026-11-04`. Compute and multiplier figures come from `data/globe-snapshot.json` (Epoch AI, file of 2026-11-04, cutoff 2026-11-01). They are Epoch estimates and must be labelled that way. They are not part of any story text.

## Why these
- **Size and compute:** GPT-2 (1.5B) → GPT-3 (175B) → Wu Dao 2.0 (1.75T) → DeepSeek-V3 (671B, mixture-of-experts) shows parameter counts climbing. The four Globe records named here (GPT-3, Wu Dao 2.0, GPT-4, GPT-6 Astra) span about 3.1×10²³ to 9.4×10²⁶ FLOP, roughly ×3,000 in six years (Epoch estimates).
- **How quickly each record fell:** each record row gives the Globe's records strip a plaque to point at. Chinchilla and o1/R1 show that the race also changed shape: more data per parameter, then reasoning trained with reinforcement learning.
- **Global spread and open weights:** five countries and nine labs (Google, OpenAI, BAAI, DeepMind, Meta, Alibaba Cloud, TII, Mistral AI, DeepSeek). Six rows are open-weight releases from the USA, France, the UAE and China, and BERT and GPT-2 show where open release began.
- Every row has a paper, a technical report or system card, or the lab's own post as its primary record.

Chinchilla carries the scaling story instead. Add it back if the owner accepts a second record I can't open.
- **GPT-3 artefact (V100):** Commons has only an NVIDIA text logo (`File:Nvidia_Tesla_V100.png`), not a photo. The GPT-3 paper says it was trained on V100 GPUs, but with no photo there is no artefact.
- **H100 artefact:** Commons has openly licensed photos (Geekerwan, CC BY 3.0, licence-reviewed). None of the 14 rows' primary sources names the H100. DeepSeek-V3 used H800s, and I found no source linking the two in a citable way.
- **BERT with the TPU artefact:** Google's post says "Cloud TPU" but does not give the version, so the TPU v3 photo is tied to Chinchilla instead.
- **Gemini 1.0 Ultra (2023), Grok 3 / Grok 4 (2025), GPT-4.5 (2025):** all are compute records in the Globe snapshot. I did not research them because of the 14-row cap. They remain on the Globe's records strip. Gemini 1.0 Ultra is the strongest substitute if a row is cut.
- **OpenAI announcement pages** (`openai.com/index/better-language-models/`, `/learning-to-reason-with-llms/`, `/gpt-6-astra/`, the localised safety overview): all returned 403. I used the verified PDFs on cdn.openai.com, the arXiv copies and the Deployment Safety Hub instead.

## Concerns
1. **Wu Dao 2.0's primary record.** I could not find a BAAI page (search only turns up press). The Tsinghua page is an official record from the lead developer's university, not from BAAI itself, and it is undated (copyright 2001–2021, "in June"). The owner should decide whether this counts as `officialPrimary`. If not, drop the row (it would leave 13). It is valuable, because it is the 2021 compute record and the first China record.
2. **Same-organisation pairs.** For GPT-2 (cdn.openai.com + github.com), GPT-4 (arxiv.org + cdn.openai.com) and o1 (arxiv.org + cdn.openai.com), both hostnames carry OpenAI's own document. That meets the hostname rule on paper but is not independent corroboration, like `evt_1985_expert_spend` in the accuracy audit. If the owner wants independence, the Epoch Atlas link could serve as a third source for GPT-2 and GPT-4. o1 has no Epoch row.
3. **GB200 artefact tie.** The link between Astra and the NVL72 rack rests on Huang's post, as reported by The Next Web. "100K+" may mean racks or GPUs, and The Next Web notes Nvidia had not clarified. The hotspot should quote only "NVLink72" racks and Grace Blackwell, not a count. I read only the first 100,000 of the system card's 216,858 characters, and that part gives no hardware.
4. **France pin.** Epoch gives Mistral 7B no compute estimate, so it is not on the Globe. Neither Mistral source I opened states the country. The story does not say "France".
5. **Dates.** The GPT-2 PDF carries no date. 2019 rests on the Epoch Atlas date (2019-02-14) and the GitHub repo. Falcon-180B's card gives no release date: the paper is from November 2023 and Epoch gives 2023-09-06, so 2023 either way. The arXiv versions of o1 and R1 were revised in 2026. The stories use the original dates.
6. **Chinchilla tokens.** The DeepMind post says 1.3 trillion tokens and the abstract says only "4× more data", so the story leaves out a token count.
